About

I'm an undergraduate at Kyung Hee University. Since September 2025 I've been a research intern at the Vision and Learning Lab, where I work with Prof. Jinwoo Choi on video understanding.

What I find interesting is the gap between what a model represents and what it can say. Motion direction is a clean example. Whether an object moved left, right, up, or down is a basic perceptual fact, and it is linearly decodable from a Video-LLM's hidden states. Yet most models answer "which way did it move?" at close to chance. My recent work traced where that signal is lost on the way to the answer, and showed that a small, targeted training objective is enough to recover it.

I like research that starts with a careful diagnosis and keeps the fix as small as the diagnosis allows.

Which Way Did It Move? Diagnosing and Overcoming Directional Motion Blindness in Video-LLMs

Jongseo Lee*, Hyuntak Lee*, Sunghun Kim, Sooa Kim, Jihoon Chung, Jinwoo Choi

NeurIPS 2026

Most Video-LLMs cannot tell which way a single object moved, even though the direction is linearly decodable from their hidden states. We trace where the signal is lost and add DeltaDirect, a small projector-level objective that predicts 2-D motion vectors from adjacent-frame feature deltas. Synthetic motion-direction accuracy goes from 25.9% to 85.4%, and real-world accuracy improves by 21.9 points with no real-world tuning data.
